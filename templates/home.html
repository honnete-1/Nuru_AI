Three ways Nuru helps

Get help straight away, without learning a system first.

Open Calm Scholar and you're asked one question: what would help you today? Every answer leads to real work, done side by side in Study Together.

organize

Assignments & study plan

Track deadlines, break an assignment into small steps, and approve a plan that fits the hours you actually have. If the work doesn't fit, you'll see it plainly, with options.

learn

Understand something

Explanations with examples, hints and worked problems. Upload your notes and answers cite the exact passage they came from.

revise

Revise with Nuru

You try a question first. Then feedback, a hint or an example, a related question, and a summary with a sensible time to review again.

support

Highs and lows

When a result comes back or things feel heavy, Nuru asks how you feel first, then lets you choose: talk, pause, look at feedback, or pick one next step.

study together

Your work in the middle, Nuru beside you

Open an assignment, choose one step, and work on it in a shared notebook. Press Help me here whenever you're stuck: Nuru sees only what you're working on, and says so. Start a focus session, mark the step done, and pick up exactly where you stopped next time.

your space

Make it yours

Choose how your seat looks, Nuru's light, the view from the window and optional ambient sound that never starts by itself.

progress

Small, honest milestones

Things for your shared desk, each tied to something you did. No streaks, no leaderboards, and nothing is taken away after a break.

Meet your study buddy

Nuru is a small lantern that helps you see the next step.

Nuru's posture and glow change with what it's doing. These are visual cues, not claims about feelings.

  • Honest about being an AI. It never pretends to be a person or to have feelings.
  • Asks one useful question at a time and lets you choose where to go next.
  • Prefers hints and your own reasoning over finished answers. It won't write graded work for you.
  • Points you towards classmates, tutors, lecturers and friends. It never guilts you for leaving.
When results come back

A 92 can disappoint. A 55 can be a turning point. So Nuru asks first.

Grades work with percentages, points or written grades. The first response follows how you say you feel, never the number. Then you choose: talk, take a pause, go through the feedback, prepare a question for your lecturer, or plan one step. Grades never feed rewards, rankings or anything public.
